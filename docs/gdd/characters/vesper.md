# Vesper

**Faction.** Kardia.  
**Role on the roster.** First measuring-stick character. Mobile bruiser. Slow for a Kardian, durable and strong from Oru nature. Brightforged tech keeps her in the fight; it does not make her a specialist peak.

**One sentence.** Vesper walks an Umbra into The Bright because her dear friend and former Brightsmith colleague defected to forge for the Aureate — and she wants revenge for that betrayal. That friend designed the Resonance Glaive she carries.

## Who she is

She was a Brightsmith before she became a Harvester. She still keeps Kardia manners: posture, etiquette, an open proud face that reads uplifting and almost static. The stoicism is performed. Oru bodies look carved and durable; inside they are fragile and loud with feeling. When emotion breaks the mask — anger, disgust, fear, joy — form slips, and she overcommits charge to close a gap and end the threat.

Kardia uphold traditions under Aureate pressure. Ego bruises them even when they know ego should not lead. Vesper carries that national pride in personal form: crest polished, coat civic, reload dignified.

## Look

Gear rules that apply to every Kardia plate also live in [armor and exosuits](../armor-and-exosuits.md): hazard gear under cloth, **20–30%** Brightforged / **70–80%** clothing, coat over exo.

**Silhouette priority.** Resonance Glaive first (this is Vesper). Shoulder **Bright-battery** second (this is Kardia). Exo braces third — visible through cutouts and coat edges, never the whole silhouette.

**Mood.** Lighter and prouder than grim survivor art. Bone, ash, and civic blue–silver under pearl Brightforged glow. Not PTSD ruin. Not a mud-caked ghost. A builder who still believes mornings can return.

**Clothes.** A literal **trenchcoat** is correct: tailored high-collared Kardia cut — off-white, faded blue, gold or silver trim — stained and worn. Resting Light **civic-modern** fashion under ash: Bright-punk grit with a modern silhouette read, not medieval costume and not Earth cyberpunk identity. Leather harness and tool belts for field work. Family crest on the chest, scratched and kept.

**Coat over the exo.** The trench wears **over** her mechanical braces. Keep the trench — do not close it into a solid cylinder that hides craft. A **deliberate cutout** is sliced from the weapon-side shoulder of the coat so the Bright-battery mount pushes through for combat reloads. Wind-splits and worn openings elsewhere let brace ribs catch light. The coat frames Brightforged; it does not bury it.

**Brightforged amount.** Cloth-first hazard kit: about **20–30%** pearl craft, **70–80%** coat and field cloth. Pearl white, faintly radiant ceramic and metal as braces, safety gauntlet, shoulder battery housing, belt spheres, and glaive hardware — **compact appliances**, not a sealed shell and not cybernetic flesh-replace. Enough to read Kardia craft. Calm contained Bright light, not Tear neon as her fuel.

### Bright-battery (must read)

The **active** Bright-battery is a pearl spherical / Core-vault-adjacent cell seated in a **shoulder mount** on her weapon-side shoulder (or the shoulder that feeds the glaive). It reads through the coat cutout as the second silhouette beat. Faint civic radiance — moonlit pearl, blue–silver hard-light accents, maybe a soft gold Core-heart wink inside glass — never Electric Cyan or magenta Tear spray as her fuel color. A short pearl tube or braided Bright-line runs from that mount toward the glaive housing so power is story-readable: battery → tool.

**Belt spheres** are the reload kit: two or three matching pearl batteries clipped with lock-and-click fittings. Same shape language as the shoulder cell, smaller or idle (dimmer). If the shoulder cell is hard to spot, the belt kit still teaches “finite Brightforged charge.”

### Exo braces and safety gauntlet

Slim pearl **brace ribs** at forearm and outer thigh / hip — the under-coat exo that keeps an Oru body in the fight and conducts Bright-battery charge. One brace should catch rim light outside the coat edge or through a split so “there is Brightforged under there” is a glance fact.

Under the ruined weapon-side cuff: a refined **Brightforged safety gauntlet** — elegant protection so she can hold the Glaive and keep Chroma off bare skin. Not a monstrous claw.

**Asymmetric Chroma wear.** The weapon-side sleeve is heavily torn, stained, and etched where Tears weep from the Resonance Glaive’s reality scratches. That sleeve looks worked and Chroma-rotted; the other side stays cleaner civic cloth.

**Tech ceiling.** Guns and lasers are in their baby shoes on Aethora; computers do not exist yet. Vesper’s answer is the glaive and finite Brightforged charge. Lit cells and tube work on her kit are Bright-battery craft, never Earth firearms or screens.

**Carry.** She walks into Umbras. The glaive rides a harness sling across the back or locks to a Brightforged hip/shoulder clip on the coat — pearl fittings that hold the housing without Earth magnets-as-sci-fi. Battery spheres sit on the belt. Tear jars and field tools hang where a mason would hang them. No vehicle fantasy on her default plate.

**Race.** Oru: taller, broader, dense alabaster skin. Exterior reads statue-strong. Interior does not.

**Reload.** Dignified civic procedure made practical by the shoulder cutout: spherical Bright-batteries on the belt swap into the exposed mount with lock-and-click pride at combat speed. Desperate slam-reload belongs to Splinter and Chroma-fuel fighters, not her.

### Hero plate checklist (keep what works)

Keep: glaive mass and magenta reality-scratch language on the blade edge only; trenchcoat civic cut worn over exo; Oru stature and carved alabaster skin; proud low-angle stoic posture; Fortiche / SD3 harvester lighting.

Change: camera or coat so the **shoulder Bright-battery** reads through a clear **cutout** as the second beat; show at least one **belt sphere**; show one **exo brace** and the **safety gauntlet** under the Chroma-worn sleeve; glaive Brightforged housing stays pearl (calm), Tears stay on the scratch/weep and the ruined cuff — do not paint her whole kit Tear-neon.

## The Resonance Glaive

A precision mason’s tool turned weapon: ultrasonic vibration wired to her battery. Brightforged metal through and through — readable as refined civic tech (clean housing, lit charge cell), not a wooden fantasy polearm. It can cut stone, metal, and — at full voice — scratch the fabric of reality. Among blades on Aethora it sits near the top of what refined Brightforged craft can do. It does not match a Splinter’s rewrite, raw Chroma burn, or a Bright Core.

**Safe Chroma contact.** Brightforged metal lets Vesper handle Chroma without bare-skin Bloom or burn. When Tears weep along the glaive, the craft takes the touch. That is why a Kardian Harvester can work Seam edges and reality scratches with a civic tool instead of dying on the first ooze.

**Reality scratches.** Side-effect of Bright vibration, not Chroma-fuel craft. Magenta/cyan Tears may weep where the cut passes. Vesper finds that ooze disgusting. She still flirts with the edge it gives her against enemies — figurative and literal — because the glaive keeps her safe enough to stay close. That tension is her moral line and her traitor lean.

On the board, scratches are not a visible lasting Seam token. Working options: dropped Bright-Tear / Chroma residue, or extra chromatic damage on the swing. Rules not settled.

## Play fantasy

**Loyal.** Mobile bruiser. Hold pressure through durability and glaive reach. Manage finite battery spheres. Finite stability: safe tech, attrition weakness when dry.

**Traitor lean.** Emotion dumps charge into gap-close to delete assassins threatening her or her team. She still reads as Kardia when she does it — manners broken, crest still on the chest.

**Bad build.** All aggression, empty spheres early, no plan when the exo goes quiet.

## Faction habit she proves

Kardia adapt. They do not own the “plant and hold the hill” fantasy — that belongs to the Aureate’s heaviest tanks. Vesper teaches adaptable Kardia play: move, brawl, reload with dignity, threaten space with a civic tool that should never have been a weapon.
