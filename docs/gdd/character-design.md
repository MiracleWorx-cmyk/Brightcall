# Character design and mechanics

A character is a person with a reason to walk an Umbra into a Bright on Aethora: four abilities, a private resource, and stats strong enough at level 1. Inside, the Bright tries to eat them. That appetite casts **Seams** onto living prey over time — volatile rewrites of tissue and fighting style. On the table those mid-match rewrites are **augments**. **Splintered** cards are a separate engineered path: Splinters driven into gear or flesh. The shared mutation deck uses Splinters.

The first measuring stick is **Vesper** (Kardia): easy to teach, durable mobile bruiser, still interesting. Full working brief: [vesper.md](characters/vesper.md). A pre-Aethora draft that leaned on graviton charges and shock damage was thrown out. Those words arrived before the factions had habits.

## The sheet

Every character sheet needs these, and nothing else until they are filled.

1. **One sentence.** Who they are in the world, and why they walked in.
2. **Faction habit.** Which row of the faction doc they prove.
3. **Base stats.** Health, physical power, ability power, movement, and the two shields at the start of round 1. These numbers must clear the level-1 line of all four abilities.
4. **Resource.** Its name, its maximum, how it comes back, and what empties it. This name never appears on a universal augment.
5. **Four slots.** One passive, two actives, one ultimate. About three card options per slot in the first set, chosen before the match.
6. **The loyal build.** The intended pre-game picks, and the turn they want to take with no lucky augment.
7. **The traitor fantasy.** The augment direction that makes them play like someone else. A bruiser who starts casting ranged ability lines. A caster who moves like a gap-closer.
8. **The bad build.** A combination that is legal and weak. This has to be written down so playtesters can recognize a failed experiment and not “fix” the character until every path works.
9. **Statuses they introduce.** Defined on their tableau. Silence is the house example: one ability, one turn.
10. **Ultimate timing.** Round 4, round 5, or a written exception.

Health regen exists unless a card says it does not. Resource regen is the character’s own rule.

## Abilities

An ability is a short base effect plus level lines. Each line is a threshold. If the dial is high enough, the line turns on. If it is not, the lower lines still work.

The reference shape is the archer sketch. It is an example of structure.

- Level 1, spent as the unlock: range 2, a small physical hit.
- Level 2: a bigger hit if physical power is 3 or more.
- Level 3: a still bigger hit if physical power is 4 or more, and a stun if ability power is 2 or more.
- Level 4: the shot repeats if movement speed is 3 or more.

Use that shape when a real card is hard to judge. Can you point at one line and say what it added? Does missing the dial feel quiet? Does any line grow a radius forever? If the answer to the last one is yes, cap it or cut it.

You unlock or upgrade one ability at the start of every turn, including round 1. The base level costs an unlock. The ultimate is not bought with the first three unlocks. It becomes available around round 4 or 5.

Once the ultimate is available it can still have level lines, and those lines compete with the other abilities for later unlocks. Something like three levels on it sits beside the wish that the full kit is online by round 15. Count the lines before you write them. Fifteen unlocks is the whole budget. Five of those turns are also augment drafts: rounds 1, 3, 6, 9, and 12.

**Passive.** Always on after its unlock, unless the card says it is a triggered passive. A passive that needs a target and a range is an active under the wrong name.

**Actives.** These are the buttons. Movement that matters should live here when the character’s choice is “dash or hit,” or on top of a small base speed if everyone needs to crawl without spending a card. That split is still open in the game-design doc. Do not give one character a free move phase and another a movement tax unless the faction habit is the reason.

**Ultimate.** The thing the table notices at round 4. It can be the identity, or the betrayal, depending on the augment already tucked. It should not be the first time the character is allowed to play.

## Stats and shields

Dials, not formulas.

**Health** starts at a small integer. The worked example in conversation was 10. Keep new characters on that scale until the guardrails doc changes it. **Physical power** feeds physical thresholds. **Ability power** feeds magical thresholds. The same ability may ask for both. **Movement** is a base speed and a threshold stat. Range 6 is the absurd reach, not a base kit. **Physical shield** and **magic shield** empty at zero. Damage of that type empties the shield first. The magic-shield example is the rule: 7 shield against 12 magic damage leaves 5 damage, which then hits health.

At the start of the turn the clock gives stat points. Augments may give more. The points from the clock are how a player chases the augment they just drafted. If the only way to reach a threshold is to draw a second stat augment, the build is linear and fails the guardrail.

Still settling: one stat point per turn, or a point on every stat. Write characters against one point per turn. That is the harsher reading, and it makes the choice of which dial to feed into the actual decision. If play is too starved, raise the clock. Do not raise it by hiding +1 damage on every augment.

## Resources

Characters may run on different fuels. That difference is flavor and a real play difference. A universal card that says “spend 3 mana” is a dead draw for the character who uses rage.

Write universal costs as “spend your resource,” or leave the cost off the augment and let the ability’s own cost stand. Regeneration is printed on the tableau: per turn, on hit, on zone, on damage taken. Pick one engine per character.

A resource that can be zero for three rounds, with no action left, is an elimination by another name. The level-1 ability has to be usable on the regen they start with.

## The pre-game build

The player picks the passive, two actives, and the ultimate from that character’s options before anyone drafts an augment. They do not draw into their identity.

Three options per slot is the first-set target. Expansions may add cards to old characters as well as new people. A new card has to say which loyal build it joins and which traitor fantasy it opens. A card that is only better than an old card, with no new angle, replaces a choice with an errand.

The pick is allowed to be greedy, safe, or incoherent. The incoherent one is part of the roster’s honesty.

## Augments on the character

Fiction: consumption Seams from the Bright’s appetite — new powers, changed fighting pattern, dangerous and incomplete eats. Two kinds, matching the two things the game wants.

**Slot cards** change one ability. They tuck under that slot. The visible edge carries the change: more range, a push, a new damage type, “this threshold reads the other dial.” Several can hang off one ability. The tail is the build, and opponents have to be able to read it without lifting cards.

**Global cards** sit beside the tableau. They are how you write “all of my ability-power lines use physical power,” or “when I heal, I may step one hex.” They cannot be forced into a single slot without lying about what they do.

A draft of three can mix both kinds. The choice is focus against breadth.

The tucked change is a modifier, not a second ability with its own four levels. If the augment needs its own cube track, it is a character card and it does not belong in the shared deck.

Tags are a tool, not a collectible. Use a small set taken from effects you already care about: physical, magic, projectile, melee, push, mark, shield, movement. A global card can say “when you push.” Do not invent a tag that only one card in the deck uses.

Swapping the dial an ability reads is one of the best augments in the game, and it is easy to overprint. Keep that trick rare enough that seeing it still feels like a heist.

## Statuses, silence, and cooldowns

Unique mechanics stay on the character. The rulebook stays thin. If a Kardia applies a bastion and a Lumari applies a brand, each tableau says what that word does. Opponents learn it by reading the board in front of them.

Silence is global enough to be a shared word: the named ability cannot be used on that character’s next activation. It does not strip levels or augments.

Cooldowns were raised and not given a system. The working answer is that cubes already gate power, and Silence already denies a button for a turn. A cooldown track on every ultimate will recreate the fizzle feeling, because the big combo is often illegal on the turn it would have mattered. Exceptions are single cubes on a single card, with the text on that card.

## Death and return

The character returns with the build. Health is full. Shields, marks, and other temporary words clear. Stat dials, levels, and Splintered cards stay. The fiction calls this Stitching. The Bright wants living matter and uses Seams to put the pattern back at the Umbra. The rule exists so a greedy build can explode, kill its owner, and still be the build they play afterward.

Stitching is not a heal-to-full in place. They come back at their Umbra, and the killer has already been paid in victory points. The exact timing is still open. Design characters so that one missed activation hurts and a deleted tableau would be a different game.

## How to know a character is ready

A character is ready for a paper prototype when all of these are true.

A new player can explain the loyal turn in two sentences. The traitor fantasy names an actual augment that exists in the test deck. The bad build is legal, and you are willing to watch someone play it. Every level-1 line fires with the printed base stats and the starting resource. No universal augment in the test deck says their resource’s name. Their statuses fit on the tableau. Their ultimate is exciting at round 4 and is not required to survive round 1. They can score without kills, or they can score with kills, and you know which plan is theirs. They are still obviously their faction when the traitor build is online. Nothing on the sheet uses a percentage or an uncapped range.

## The first one

**Vesper** is the measuring stick, not the most original kit in the box. Detail: [characters/vesper.md](characters/vesper.md).

Easy to teach. Durable mobile bruiser. Slow for a Kardian; Oru mass and finite Bright cells keep her in the brawl. She does not peak like a specialist. Kardia as a faction are adaptable. They do not own plant-and-hold. The Aureate own the heaviest objective tanks.

Loyal loop: glaive pressure, dignified Bright cell reloads, space threat without a hill mandate. Traitor fantasy for the prototype: emotion overcommits charge into a real gap-close to delete assassins. (Chroma-edge flirt from reality scratches — Seams almost opening — is flavor and a possible damage line; she does not burn Chroma-fuel.) Bad build: all three aggressive cards, cells empty early, no plan when the exo goes quiet. Silhouette: Resonance Glaive first, shoulder Bright cell second (through the coat cutout). Proud open face, not PTSD ruin. Cloth-first hazard gear: [armor and exosuits](armor-and-exosuits.md). Leading plate: `site/public/art/hero-harvester.png`.

After Vesper survives one session, the second Kardia widens adaptable tools, and the first Refract proves the schism in the cards. Everyone else waits.
